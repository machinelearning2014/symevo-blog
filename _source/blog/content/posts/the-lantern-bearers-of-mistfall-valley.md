# The Lantern-Bearers of Mistfall Valley

*A still, near-evening valley. Terraced green climbs into pine ridges; hundreds of small paper lanterns hang from long, sagging ropes strung between the trees, and the mist below turns their light into a soft amber haze. A single figure stands on the ridge trail with a lantern of their own.*

## What the picture holds

- **Three depth planes.** A near ridge in deep teal, a mid-water of luminous mist, and a far mountain that has almost dissolved into the sky.
- **A single warm light source repeated.** Every lantern is the same tiny shape, so the eye moves along the ropes like beads on a necklace.
- **One point of human scale.** The watcher on the trail is the only vertical silhouette, and their lantern answers the nearest hanging light.
- **A quiet palette.** Muted teal and slate blues for everything receding, with gold, amber, and rose only where the lanterns are.

## Notes on making it

The composition is built from simple primitives — layered silhouettes, soft radial glows, and a scattering of small repeated forms — which keeps the dusk gradients smooth while the lantern network stays legible at a distance. Nothing in the scene is copied from an existing artwork; the valley, the ridge line, and the lantern strings were placed by hand for balance along a diagonal from the lower left to the upper right.

## How to look at it

Stand back first and read the diagonal of light. Then step closer: the mist has slow horizontal bands, the far mountain has a thin rim of pale rose where the last daylight catches it, and the ropes carry a slight sag so they never read as straight rules across the sky.
