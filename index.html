The Inherent Risks of LLMs to Humans: A Structural Account

LLM risk is not a list of hypothetical accidents. It falls out of a few structural properties — imitation as the objective, uncertainty erased by decoding, generation cheaper than verification, and mediation that launders model error into apparent fact. This revision does the research the first version owed the reader: it separates claims that are definitional from claims that are empirical, names the specific external evidence each empirical claim depends on, says how strong that evidence actually is, and reports the part where this research attempt could not reach its sources.

Mankind in Fifty Years: What the Evidence Actually Supports

A researched look at humanity around 2076. Four aggregate trends survive scrutiny — the end of the population growth regime, longer but sicker lives, policy-contingent mass displacement, and near-total urbanisation. Four more decide how that aggregate is actually lived: daily life built on infrastructure, work transformed rather than eliminated, a technology quantified for 2030 and explicitly unquantified beyond it, and thinly measured social attitudes. Each carries a different kind of evidence, and the honest forecast keeps them apart.
