"Enter wide" is a cornering-technique instruction with two parts. *Enter* names the start of the turn. *Wide* names the car's lateral position there — toward the outside of the available road, away from the central island — so the arc through the turn is shallower and the steering input is smaller and more constant.

It does **not** mean staying on the outer edge for the whole circulation. It is a transient position at the entry only.

## Yes to the direction, no to the permanence

Enter wide does mean the outer side rather than the inner side, so it is correctly opposed to hugging the inner circle next to the island. What the word *hug* gets wrong is the permanence: entering wide is a momentary placement at the start of the turn, and the aim is a smooth outside-to-inside-to-outside sweep, not a sustained hug of the outer circle.

## Three paths, only one of which is "enter wide"

```
        EXIT (top)  ->  car returns OUTSIDE
                 \    |    /
                  \   |   /
   OUTER EDGE  . . \. .|. ./ . .  <- widest point again at your exit
                .    (     )    .
   MID CIRCLE  .    (  ISLAND )    .
                .    (     )    .
   INNER EDGE  . . . (     ) . . .  <- closest to island, mid-circulation
                     |     |
                     |     |
                 ENTRY (bottom)  <- car starts WIDE, away from island
```

**Path A — "enter wide" (the correct one): one smooth sweep**

```
                2.............3
              .                 .
             .    ( ISLAND )     .
              .                 .
               1               4
   1  ENTRY: car placed WIDE, outer side, away from the island   <- momentary only
   2  sweeps INWARD past the intermediate exits
   3  apex near the island at the crown of the turn
   4  unwinds OUTWARD again to the outer side at your own exit
   The line is unbroken and single-phase. Position 1 is a point, not a state.
```

**Path B — "hug the outer circle" (what the wording wrongly implies)**

```
        X X X X X X X X X X X X
      X                           X
      X        ( ISLAND )         X
      X                           X
        X X X X X X X X X X X X
   The car stays pinned to the OUTER edge the whole way round:
   it never moves inward (no step 2, no step 3). It is still
   circulating in the outer lane at your exit instead of turning out.
   Same side as step 1 — but permanent, which is the error.
```

**Path C — "hug the inner circle next to the island" (the opposite error)**

```
                (ISLAND)
              o o o o o o
            o               o
            o    tight      o
            o    radius     o
              o o o o o o
   Radius held small the whole way round; the car is turned in early
   and the wheel is held hard over, so it must be corrected late.
   This is directionally opposite to step 1, not a variant of it.
```

**The correction in one line:** Path A touches the outside only at its *start and its end*. Path B stays there. Path A is what "enter wide" instructs; Path B is what the word *hug* wrongly suggests.

## Why it matters for grip

The purpose of entering wide is grip management, not appearance. A tyre can only give so much total force: whatever it spends holding the car on its curved path, it cannot spend on driving or braking. The flattened arc of a wide entry keeps the steering input smooth and single-phase, and avoids the sharp, late, mid-corner correction that drives tyres to their grip limit. Both errors fail to deliver that economy of steering — the outer hug never uses the room the geometry offers, and the inner hug holds a tight radius that must be corrected late.

This is the same mechanism behind the common hybrid complaint of a car that feels like it skids when coasting through a roundabout but grips when light throttle is held: lifting off commands regenerative braking on the front axle, which is already carrying most of the cornering load, so the front tyres must produce lateral grip and longitudinal drag at once and saturate early.

## What a static diagram cannot show

A drawn figure of this kind is static and schematic. It cannot animate the car's motion, cannot show the *timing and rate* of the steering input, and is not to scale for any particular roundabout. The momentary character of the wide entry is carried by the entry position being a single marked point on an unbroken sweep line — not by movement.

A diagram also cannot convey the limits under which the technique holds: reduce the approach speed further on wet, greasy or contaminated tarmac, when the car is heavily loaded, and when tyre pressures or tread are not at spec. Light steady throttle is a smoothness technique, not extra grip.
